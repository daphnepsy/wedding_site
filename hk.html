Chinese Wedding Banquet Etiquette

  • Red envelopes: Cash gifts in a red envelope (lai see / hongbao) are the traditional gift — no registry needed.
  • Arrive on time: Banquets run on a schedule, with the couple's entrance and toasts kicking things off.
  • Multi-course meal: Expect a long, multi-course banquet (often 8-10 courses) served over a few hours.
  • Toasting: Guests will be invited to toast the couple throughout the meal — a hearty "yum sing!" ("drink for victory") is traditional.
  • What to wear: Bright, festive colors are welcome; it's best to avoid all-white or all-black outfits, which are associated with mourning.

The Tea Ceremony

Earlier in the day, Daphne and Conner will take part in a traditional tea ceremony, kneeling before their parents and elders and serving them tea as a formal show of respect and gratitude. In return, elders offer blessings (and often a red envelope!) to the couple. This is typically a smaller, family-only moment before the banquet.
